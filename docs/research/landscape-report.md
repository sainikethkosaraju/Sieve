# Sieve's gap is measurement, not cascades

**Bottom line.** A "calibrated, abstention-aware small-to-large cascade" is not novel: at least five works from 2024-2026 already combine cascades with calibration, abstention, conformal guarantees or harm-aware escalation, so Sieve cannot claim the algorithm. What appears open is the integration and the evidence: no packaged, OpenAI-compatible, locally runnable tool does post-hoc confidence-based escalation plus abstention on llama.cpp/Ollama-class backends, and no paper found reports risk-coverage-cost curves with measured latency, VRAM and energy on consumer GPUs, or how quantization, KV-cache quantization and speculative decoding change the confidence gate. The full project as scoped (cascade, retrieval escalation, hidden-state probes, speculative decoding plus quantized KV, vLLM and llama.cpp benchmarks, upstream PRs, preprint, workshop paper, 300+ stars in about 14 weeks, solo) is too wide; I rate it over-ambitious as stated and achievable if narrowed. My recommended scope is a thin proxy over llama-server/Ollama with two tiers plus abstain, cheap token-level signals and optional self-consistency, a per-model-per-quantization calibration tool, and a cached-output evaluation harness that measures real hardware cost. Hidden-state probes, retrieval and any engine-level inference work should be cut or reduced to offline studies and configuration. **Confidence caveat:** arxiv.org, docs.vllm.ai and info.arxiv.org were unreachable, so nearly every 2025-2026 paper claim below is from search snippets, not full texts. Novelty conclusions are hypotheses until the six papers in the final table are read.

Legend used below: **[Fetched]** means a primary page (repo, policy file, blog) was read in the research session; **[Snippet]** means a search-result summary of a paper or page; **[Inference]** is my or the researchers' reasoning; **[Memory]** is background knowledge with no source this session.

## The cascade-plus-abstention core is already crowded

The foundational routing literature (FrugalGPT, AutoMix, Hybrid LLM, RouteLLM, RouterBench) measures cost in API dollars or call fractions and treats quality as accuracy; none of these treats abstention as an output ([Not-Diamond awesome list](https://github.com/Not-Diamond/awesome-ai-model-routing)) [Snippet, with venues from Memory]. The newer line is the threat. Early-Abstention cascades let small models abstain on queries a larger model would also fail, reporting about a 4.1% absolute average abstention-rate gain ([arXiv 2502.09054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09054)). HCMA builds a hierarchical chain using token probabilities for routing and abstention with an improved Platt scaling ([arXiv 2410.02173](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02173)). UCCI aggregates token margins, applies isotonic regression, reports ECE 0.03 on its workload and routes under a cost constraint ([arXiv 2605.18796](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18796)). Conformal Cascade uses prediction-set size as the deferral signal, claims a finite-sample accuracy guarantee, and argues confidence thresholds must be tuned per model pair and domain ([arXiv 2607.25018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25018)). Signed Rescue Routing trains separate "rescue" and "harm" heads on cheap runtime features of the small model, so escalation accounts for cases where the big model is worse ([arXiv 2609.07786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07786)). All five are [Snippet]-level: no method details, tables or code were seen. Adjacent work includes ReDAct's uncertainty-gated deferral for agents with roughly 15% large-model calls ([arXiv 2604.07036](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07036v1)) and the NeurIPS 2025 base-model, large-model, human cascade with explicit deferral and abstention policies ([arXiv 2506.11887](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11887v2)). R-Tuning covers trained refusal ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.394)).

On the hardware-grounded cost axis the picture is thinner. Measured Joules, Learned Routes trains a router on measured GPU energy and reports lower energy at comparable accuracy, but its snippet mentions neither abstention nor calibration guarantees ([arXiv 2609.23085](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23085)). Edge-Cascade is an anecdotal laptop project cascading NPU, a 14B model on an RTX 5070 Ti via Ollama, and Claude cloud, gated by a code verifier ([repo](https://github.com/danthemanvsqz/Edge-Cascade)) [Fetched]. Related edge-energy papers establish that small models are far cheaper per token (about 7x energy per token for 1.5B versus 14B) ([arXiv 2511.01866](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01866)) [Snippet] but are not cascades. The researchers' verdict, which I share, is that Sieve's defensible novelty is **the integration plus an evaluation protocol: risk-coverage together with measured latency, VRAM and energy on consumer GPUs through a reproducible serving stack** [Inference, moderate-low confidence]. Two items deserve specific emphasis. First, a cascade that only escalates on uncertainty ignores large-model errors, which is exactly Signed Rescue's point; Sieve should abstain when neither tier is trusted. Second, VRAM co-residency and model-swap cost in cascades was found in no source, which makes it a genuinely unexamined consumer-hardware variable [Inference; absence of evidence from limited search]. The latest items found date from September 2026; nothing from October-November 2026, Semantic Scholar citation graphs or ICLR 2027 OpenReview submissions was checked.

## Open source has infrastructure but no confidence-aware router

The engines are huge and every one speaks the OpenAI protocol: Ollama about 185.7k stars, llama.cpp about 133.2k, vLLM about 96.2k, SGLang about 38.4k (all [Fetched] 2026-11-03 from [llama.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp), [vLLM](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm), [Ollama](https://github.com/ollama/ollama), [SGLang](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang)). They are backends for Sieve, not competitors, and none ships routing or abstention policy. The routers and gateways route on other things. RouteLLM (about 5.7k stars) uses a prompt-based preference router and claims up to 85% cost reduction at 95% of GPT-4 quality on MT-Bench ([repo](https://github.com/lm-sys/RouteLLM)). vLLM Semantic Router (about 6.4k stars, v0.4 dated 2026-09-24) routes on task fit, compute and cost signals and includes hallucination detection and semantic caching, with no confidence-escalation or abstention described ([repo](https://github.com/vllm-project/semantic-router)). LiteLLM (about 62.6k) offers fallbacks and a heuristic complexity router ([repo](https://github.com/BerriAI/litellm)); Portkey (about 13.3k) offers guardrail-triggered retries and conditional routing ([repo](https://github.com/Portkey-AI/gateway)); Arch/Plano (about 7.1k) uses a 4B routing model ([repo](https://github.com/katanemo/archgw)). All [Fetched], through a page summarizer that did not capture release dates or last commits.

The researchers' taxonomy, which I accept, is prompt-predicted difficulty, operational policy, and output guardrails; **model-internal confidence as an escalation trigger is absent from all of them** [Inference]. Small confidence-adjacent repos exist but with negligible adoption: the NeurIPS cascade code has 1 star and needs an A100 80GB ([repo](https://github.com/fanconic/cascaded-llms)), confidence-escalation middleware targets agent actions ([repo](https://github.com/ashutoshrana/confidence-escalation)), and others are cloud-only or prompt-only ([LLM-Model-Cascade](https://github.com/Anupbhagat77/LLM-Model-Cascade), [hallucinationControl](https://github.com/binzeli/hallucinationControl)). The whitespace claim rests on one extended search, GitHub topic pages were not enumerated, and **no user-demand evidence (HN, Reddit, issues) was gathered**, which is a real gap: whitespace can mean no market. Two structural risks follow. Post-hoc escalation pays for the failed local attempt, whereas prompt-based routers add near-zero generation cost, so Sieve must show its quality gain is worth the extra latency (the one cited practitioner figure of 2-8 ms embedding routers versus 150-450 ms small-LLM routers is from an unverified blog ([llms.blog](https://www.llms.blog/posts/model-routing-and-cascades-in-production-comparing-routellm-frugalgpt-embedding-classifiers-and-verifier-cascades))). And vLLM Semantic Router already carries hallucination detection under the vLLM brand and could expand into this niche.

## Cheap token signals ship today; hidden-state probes do not

What Sieve can compute depends on the backend. llama-server's `/completion` supports `n_probs` and `/chat/completions` accepts `logprobs` and `top_logprobs` ([llama.cpp commit](https://cdn04132025.gitlink.org.cn/replica/llama.cpp/commit/57bb2c40cd94c5a09f5210ed8264cc93b21c4b7e)) [Snippet; current README unchecked]. vLLM returns logprobs of generated tokens, possibly not reflecting the final adjusted sampling distribution, while hidden states are exposed only on the separate pooling path ([vLLM forum](https://discuss.vllm.ai/t/how-to-obtain-the-logits-of-llm/847), [vLLM outputs docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/api/vllm/outputs/)) [Snippet]. From these servers Sieve can derive max probability, top-N entropy (not exact full-vocabulary entropy), top-1 minus top-2 margin, mean and length-normalized logprob, k-sample consistency and verbalized or P(True) prompts [Inference]. Hidden-state probes (SAPLMA-style, semantic entropy probes), EigenScore and LLM-Check need HF Transformers or custom engine hooks, and llama.cpp's hidden-state API was not located [Inference; Gap]. That is the decisive constraint for the "hidden-state probes" goal: it conflicts with the llama.cpp-backend design unless probes run in a separate Transformers path.

On signal quality, the evidence is mixed and mostly indirect. Verbalized confidence beat raw token probabilities for RLHF API models, often roughly halving ECE ([Tian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975v2)), but verbalized confidence is generally overconfident ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)) and reasoning models are often above 85% confident even when wrong ([Mei et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18183v3)); no data covered sub-8B or quantized models [Snippet; the small-model claim is my hypothesis]. Semantic entropy detects confabulations by clustering sampled answers, costs roughly 5-10x one generation and misses consistently wrong answers by design ([OATML](https://oatml.cs.ox.ac.uk/blog/2024/06/19/detecting_hallucinations_2024.html), [Nature paper via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11186750)) [Fetched/secondary]. Semantic entropy probes approximate it from one pass with a linear probe, transferring better to unseen tasks than accuracy probes but not beating full sampling ([arXiv 2406.15927](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.15927)); Kadavath et al. found P(IK) calibration transfers poorly to new tasks ([arXiv 2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.05221)). A May 2026 single preprint claims 0.904-1.000 AUROC from a mid-layer probe on quantized models versus at most 0.541 for sampling methods, but on a paired true/false statement protocol rather than generation, so it should be treated as protocol-dependent and unreplicated ([arXiv 2606.02628](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02628)) [Snippet]. The LM-Polygraph toolkit already implements many of these baselines (token entropy, sequence probability, semantic entropy, verbalized) across 11 tasks and is the obvious reuse target rather than reimplementing them ([TACL](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.11.pdf)) [Snippet]; I could not verify its overall conclusion about whether simple baselines are competitive. Head-to-head AUROC numbers for entropy versus margin versus length-normalized likelihood on modern instruct models were not found, so Sieve's signal ranking is an empirical question it must answer itself.

Calibration is the quiet center of the project. Conformal factuality offers marginal distribution-free guarantees ([Mohri and Hashimoto](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mohri24a.html)), but guarantees are average-case and need calibration data exchangeable with deployment data [Inference]. Quantization effects are contested: GPTQ 4-bit lowers confidence in the true label, concentrated on already-uncertain samples ([arXiv 2405.00632](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.00632)); a 2025 study finds no substantial calibration change at 4 or 8 bits ([arXiv 2508.16785](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16785)); a July 2026 claim puts reliability peak at 4-bit ([alphaxiv 2607.10855](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.10855.md)) [all Snippet]. Nothing found studies GGUF K-quants, FP8 or quantized KV on calibration, which is the clearest empirical hole adjacent to Sieve [Inference]. The practical rule is that thresholds do not transfer between an FP16 model and its 4-bit GGUF, so calibration must be per model, per quantization and per domain.

## An evaluation suite that can survive reviewers

The benchmark recommendation is tiered. SimpleQA Verified (1,000 prompts, about 77% of the original removed for label and bias problems, F1 headline) anchors parametric factual QA ([arXiv 2509.07968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07968)); PopQA and TriviaQA subsamples add statistical power; AbstentionBench (20 datasets, NeurIPS 2025) tests whether abstention generalizes beyond unknown answers, and reports that reasoning fine-tuning degrades abstention by 24% on average and scaling does not help ([arXiv 2506.09038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09038)) [Snippet]; MMLU-Redux replaces MMLU, whose error rate is about 6.5% ([arXiv 2406.04127](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04127)). AbstentionBench's finding implies that if Sieve's big tier is a reasoning model, abstention must be evaluated explicitly [Inference]. Dataset licences were not resolved reliably (NQ, SQuAD 2.0 and TriviaQA licence claims conflict across secondary sources) and must be checked on Hugging Face dataset cards. Several 2026 benchmarks (PhantomBench, "Two Axes of LLM Abstention", "The Missing I Don't Know", PARALLAX's claim that 4 of 6 hallucination datasets leak ground truth) are known only from aggregator abstracts and should not be cited until verified ([emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.11105), [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.17686)).

Grading and metrics carry the credibility. SimpleQA's three-way correct, incorrect, not-attempted scheme uses a prompted grader that produced false negatives in 15 of 56 manually inspected "incorrect" labels ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-simpleqa/)), and a Korean adaptation found autoraters over-assign NOT_ATTEMPTED to false refusals and degenerate text ([arXiv 2510.18368](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18368)); because that inflates apparent abstention quality, Sieve should use alias-matched exact match first, a pinned cross-family judge second, a separate explicit abstention rule, and a human-labelled audit sample [Inference]. For selective metrics, AURC has documented flaws and AUGRC was proposed to replace it, changing metric rankings on 5 of 6 datasets in a general-classification study ([arXiv 2407.01032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01032)); I recommend reporting coverage, selective accuracy, hallucination rate (both unconditional answered-and-wrong and conditional error-among-answered, named distinctly), abstention precision and recall, AUROC, AUGRC, Brier and equal-mass-bin ECE, with cost per correct answer. For routing curves, RouterBench uses AIQ with Zero-Router and Oracle baselines ([arXiv 2403.12031](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12031v2)) and RouteLLM uses APGR and CPT ([LMSYS](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-07-01-routellm/)); neither has an abstain action, so Sieve must extend them, for example with a second panel of hallucination rate against cost [Inference]. Statistically, Miller's error-bar guidance supports paired question-level differences and clustered standard errors ([arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00640)); by my arithmetic n=1,000 resolves only about ±3.1pp, so SimpleQA Verified alone cannot show small gains and selective metrics at low coverage need question-level bootstrap with at least 1,000 resamples. Releasing cached per-item outputs for every model makes curves recomputable offline, which is both a reproducibility win and the cheapest way to let others add routers. Tooling (Inspect AI versus lm-eval-harness versus a thin custom harness) was decided from secondary sources only and should be settled after reading their docs.

## Efficient-inference building blocks are available upstream; Sieve should configure and measure them

Speculative decoding is already broad in the engines. llama.cpp lists `--spec-type` values including draft-simple, EAGLE-3, DFlash, DSpark, MTP and several n-gram modes, with defaults of `--spec-draft-n-max` 3, and ships a SPEED-Bench client and synthetic-acceptance flags but **no published performance numbers** ([speculative.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/docs/speculative.md)) [Fetched]. vLLM supports EAGLE, MTP, draft models, n-gram and suffix methods ([docs, search summary](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/speculative_decoding/)) [Snippet]. A third-party experiment suggests speculation pays off above roughly 60-70% acceptance ([dev.to](https://dev.to/cyprus09/is-speculative-decodings-speedup-a-hardware-problem-or-a-model-problem-7k6)) [unverified, single experiment], and llama-server was reportedly 10-15% slower than the standalone speculative binary in one case ([issue 12968](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/12968)) [status unknown]. No verified consumer-GPU speedups for EAGLE-3 or DFlash were found. A point worth stating clearly: standard speculative decoding preserves the target distribution, so it should leave confidence signals unchanged in principle while changing latency [Inference, from general method properties; verify empirically since the docs note CPU versus backend sampling can diverge numerically]. Implementing a new speculative method would be research in its own right and a distraction.

KV-cache quantization is the more interesting interaction. q8_0 is widely described as near-lossless and q4_0 as riskier at long context, but the evidence is anecdotal blogs ([llmhardware.io](https://llmhardware.io/guides/kv-cache-quantization-ollama-llama-cpp)) [low confidence]; the strongest primary source is vLLM's FP8 KV write-up, where 128k needle-in-a-haystack accuracy fell from 91% to 13% on Hopper until a two-level accumulation fix restored 89% ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-04-22-fp8-kvcache)) [Fetched]. One blog reports q8_0 KV leaving perplexity nearly unchanged while changing token choices ([dev.to](https://dev.to/breachprotocol/quantizing-v4-flashs-kv-cache-in-llamacpp-changes-which-tokens-it-picks-38np)). Token-choice flips are exactly what perturbs entropy and margin gates, so a confidence-centered KV-quant study is something nobody found has published [Inference]. Weight-quantization rankings are model-dependent (GGUF K and I-quants roughly comparable to AWQ with GPTQ often trailing, from blogs of uncertain provenance: [computingforgeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/gguf-vs-awq-vs-gptq/)); Sieve should report KL divergence and top-1 agreement alongside task accuracy rather than perplexity alone.

For benchmarking against vLLM and llama.cpp there is no authoritative joint methodology. `llama-bench` and `vllm bench serve` measure different things and are not directly comparable, so the guidance is to compare server to server under identical concurrency, matched precision, warm-up and repeated runs, logging build flags and commits ([markaicode](https://markaicode.com/benchmarks/vllm-vs-llamacpp-performance/)) [third-party]. Because vLLM and llama.cpp are backends and baselines, "benchmarked against" should mean Sieve-over-backend versus the bare backend, not a race against engines [Inference]. Energy measurement is feasible but fiddly: nvidia-smi readings can be improved by up to 35% with proper practice ([arXiv 2312.02741](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02741v3)), NVML energy counters reportedly repeat values at sub-100 ms intervals, and CodeCarbon's total error was 10.3% against a wall meter in one study ([arXiv 2505.15631](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15631)). Plan on integrating sampled power at 10 Hz or more, subtracting idle, and validating at least one configuration with a wall meter. Hardware envelopes (7-8B at Q4 on 8 GB, up to about 20B on 16 GB) come from low-quality conflicting sources and must be checked against GGUF file sizes plus KV; 24 GB, Apple Silicon and CPU-only envelopes were not researched.

## Upstream, paper and launch targets are reachable only with discipline

Upstream policy is strict about AI use. llama.cpp permits AI-generated code but threatens permanent bans for undisclosed use, prohibits AI-written issue and PR text, limits new contributors to one open PR, rejects trivial fixes, and requires a reproducible issue plus a regression test for bug fixes ([CONTRIBUTING.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)) [Fetched]. vLLM requires disclosure, DCO sign-off, an RFC for changes over 500 LOC, and caps non-write contributors at 6 open PRs ([contributing README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/vllm/main/docs/contributing/README.md)) [Fetched]. transformers asks first-time contributors not to use code agents ([CONTRIBUTING.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)) [Fetched]. The best-fitting live opportunities, queried on 2026-11-03, are llama-server logprobs bugs ([#31451](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/31451) dropped logprobs for multi-token UTF-8 characters; [#29456](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/29456) uncapped `n_probs`; [#31206](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/31206) nondeterministic /v1/completions logprobs) and the lm-evaluation-harness request for an RMS calibration error metric ([#2189](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/issues/2189)) and paired-significance bug ([#3831](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/issues/3831)). Entropy-gated speculative decoding requests ([#29875](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/29875), [#27821](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/27821)) show community interest in entropy inside llama.cpp, but a new server-side confidence API would face the "higher bar" for public APIs and should start as an issue. The logprobs fixes are the right credibility play because they are bugs in the exact signal Sieve depends on, which gives each PR a motivation that is honest rather than résumé padding. No first-time-contributor merge case studies and no merge-latency statistics were gathered.

On publication, the realistic target is an ICLR 2027 workshop: the suggested contribution deadline is 1 February 2027 with acceptance notifications by 26 February, but each workshop sets its own deadline and the venue list was not retrieved ([ICLR 2027 dates](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/Dates)); sources conflict on location. The MLSys 2027 deadline (30 October 2026) and UncertaiNLP at EMNLP 2026 have already passed ([mlsys.org](https://mlsys.org), [ACL portal](https://www.aclweb.org/portal/content/3rd-workshop-uncertainty-aware-nlp-emnlp-2026)). First-time arXiv submitters to a category need an endorsement from an established author in that category ([arXiv help, snippet](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html)); start that search now since it is outside the builder's control. Fourteen weeks from today ends around 9 February 2027, so the paper must essentially be written before the repo is polished. On launch, the only controlled analysis found says Hacker News posting time matters and the "Show HN" tag adds nothing after controls ([arXiv 2511.04453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04453v1)); postmortems report 3,000 stars in two months for a company-backed tool ([Infisical](https://infisical.com/blog/how-we-got-3000-github-stars-in-2-months.md)) and a Product Hunt-to-Trending jump after 1,000+ slow days ([ExpertBeacon](https://expertbeacon.com/how-i-took-an-open-source-project-from-0-to-3000-stars-a-data-driven-post-mortem/)), both self-reported. r/LocalLLaMA norms (roughly one self-promotion post in ten, karma minimums) come from a third-party wiki mirror and must be checked live ([mirror](https://r.datuan.dev/r/LocalLLaMA/wiki/index)). The pattern from RouteLLM's README, a quantified headline plus a drop-in OpenAI client, is the template to copy ([repo](https://github.com/lm-sys/RouteLLM)), though RouteLLM and vLLM Semantic Router borrow distribution from lm-sys and vLLM, which a solo builder lacks.

## How ambitious is it, and what to build instead

The researchers' unsourced priors, which I adopt as rough judgment only, are: at least one merged PR in llama.cpp or lm-eval about 60-70% (20-40 hours), three merged PRs about 15%, a vLLM or transformers merge about 30%, a workshop acceptance about 35-45% (80-120 hours), and 300 stars about 25-35% without a breakout post. These are not base rates from data. Multiplying even the optimistic ones shows the combined goal set is a low-probability bundle for one person, and the bundled engineering scope makes it worse: building a cascade, three signal families, retrieval, an OpenAI-compatible server, speculative decoding with quantized KV, two-engine benchmarks, a paper and a launch is several quarters of work for a team. The failure modes I see are: being scooped or reviewer-dismissed on algorithmic novelty by Conformal Cascade, UCCI or Signed Rescue; a single-GPU result that reviewers call anecdotal; thresholds that do not transfer across models, quantizations or domains; the probe path forcing a Transformers dependency that breaks the "runs on llama.cpp" story; and a launch with no demo or no baselines.

My recommended scope is the following. Build Sieve as a thin OpenAI-compatible proxy over llama-server and Ollama with two local tiers plus an abstain action, optionally with one cloud or larger-local escalation tier. Support token-level signals (entropy over top-N, margin, mean logprob) and optional k=3 self-consistency, with LM-Polygraph reused as a reference for offline comparison. Ship a `sieve calibrate` command that fits isotonic or temperature scaling on a held-out split per model and quantization and emits a threshold for a target hallucination rate. Ship an evaluation harness with cached outputs, question-level bootstrap and the metric set above, run on SimpleQA Verified, PopQA/TriviaQA and an AbstentionBench subset, and measure latency, VRAM and wall-meter-validated energy on one or two consumer GPUs. Make the paper's claim about two things: the measured-cost risk-coverage frontier, and how weight quantization and KV-cache quantization move the gate's quality, treating speculative decoding as a latency knob expected to be calibration-neutral. Include Signed Rescue-style reasoning by measuring cases where the larger tier is wrong and abstaining when neither is trusted. Cut or defer: hidden-state probes (run as an offline HF-based ablation if time allows), retrieval escalation (a stub interface only), any engine-level speculative or KV work, and a vLLM-versus-llama.cpp engine comparison beyond using vLLM as a backend. Pair this with two to three small upstream contributions that serve the signal pipeline (the logprobs bug fixes and the lm-eval calibration metric), each issue-first and hand-written.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "is a calibrated abstaining cascade new?" (it is not) to "who has measured one honestly on the hardware real people own?" The answer appears to be no one, and that framing is better for all three goals: it makes the preprint a measurement paper that does not depend on out-algorithming UCCI or Conformal Cascade, it makes upstream PRs on logprobs correctness a natural by-product of the signal pipeline, and it gives the launch a quantified headline of the kind that carried RouteLLM, for example hallucination rate and joules per correct answer at fixed coverage on a stated GPU.

Two findings are non-obvious and worth acting on early. Quantization and KV-cache quantization are plausibly the largest unmeasured source of threshold drift, so calibration should be a per-deployment build step, not a shipped constant. And the honest risk is not engineering but evidence: until the six threatening papers are read and a demand check is done, every novelty and market claim is a hypothesis, so the first week should be spent reading, not coding.
